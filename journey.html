My i.c.stars Journey

Over 14 weeks in i.c.stars Cycle 60, I grew as a leader, a business thinker and a technologist. These reflections cover my experiences in Leadership, Business, Technology, Career Planning and Wellness.

Leadership

Team Week

Team Week was my first real chance to meet every intern in Cycle 60, and it changed how I saw the people around me. In conversation after conversation, I learned that everyone had walked a different path to get here. Some had come through real hardships, and all of us arrived with our own goals for what this internship could become. Hearing those stories made me see the cohort as a group of people with something to prove, not just classmates.

When we were assigned to teams, I met my group, All Stars Associates (ASA): Andrew Thomas, Freeda Garcia, Raymond Hall and Ben Coppage. Each of them brings something brilliant and different to the table. When we divided up roles for our Medline client project, the Operations Problem Navigator RFP, I stepped up to take the UI/UX lead role, taking ownership of making sure what we designed would be clear and easy for the people who'd actually use it. Over the course of Team Week, those working relationships turned into a real bond, one that has carried through the rest of the program.

Gift Project: The Tech Tree

The Gift Project is meant to be a gift to the Cycle 60 cohort and to the i.c.stars family we've built together. I worked with Briana Heredia and Faiza Baloch, and we went through several rounds of brainstorming, pushing each other to find an idea that was meaningful and brought together technology and art.

We eventually came together behind an idea Briana proposed: a tech tree. Backing a teammate's idea instead of holding on to my own taught me that sometimes the best way to lead is to get fully behind someone else's vision. I bought the plant pot and soil and helped build the tree together with the team. Its branches are LAN cables signed by cohort members, staff and alumni, built up with spare computer parts, a metal pole and zip ties, and planted in a pot of soil. Everyone in the cohort had a hand in it, along with some staff and alumni, so the finished piece belongs to all of us.

I couldn't have asked for better teammates. Each of us brought our own input, and that mix of ideas is what made the final result so strong.

Community Roles

The Plant. My first community role was The Plant, which I shared with my partner Mariia Kit. The role is simple on paper: water the plants in the studio, keep them alive and healthy, and if there aren't any, figure out how to get some. Mariia and I took it further, caring for the plants already in the office and working to bring in more greenery to make the space feel more alive.

My love of animals and plants caught the attention of Brandies, our Program Director, who gave me a special project: to design a vivarium/terrarium that would house a tortoise mascot among living plants. It would sit on the office's wide window ledge as something for future interns to look at and be motivated by. I'm now in the research phase, choosing a tortoise species that fits the space, picking safe plants, and figuring out what kind of enclosure will keep the mascot healthy long-term.

Cycle Wellness Lead. My second role chose me. As Cycle Wellness Lead, my job was to share wellness resources, encourage healthy habits during an intense program, and help make it normal to take care of yourself while doing demanding work. Knowing how much our training schedule asks of us, especially for cohort members commuting from across the West and South Sides, I put together a guide of free wellness activities planned around our schedule: midweek options for our Wednesday 2:00 PM early release, like Nature Therapy at Garfield Park Conservatory and Healing Arts Chicago workshops, and weekend options like the Millennium Park Fitness Series and Chicago Park District wellness programs. I included times, requirements and sign-up links so anyone could plan a solo reset or team up with other cohort members. I also made a point to check in with cohort members, staff and alumni about how they were really doing, listening and sharing encouragement when it helped, while knowing when to point someone toward professional support.

Tea Host

My first time serving as a Tea Host was for Tamara Draper, during a virtual tea. To prepare, I researched her background on LinkedIn and came in with questions about her career path and what she did in each of her roles. I learned about her work in financial crime prevention and relationship management, a field I hadn't thought much about before, and it showed me how many different paths a career can take. One of my favorite moments was when she shared that she and her daughter have gone to high teas all over the world looking for the very best one, with Canada and Dubai as top contenders. It was a fitting story for a tea, and a reminder that the most successful people still make time for the moments that matter to them.

Tea Debriefs

The Tea Debriefs throughout the program shaped how I think. Again and again, I heard from people who started with nothing and built their careers, their lives and the relationships they made along the way into success, and who now use their knowledge, leadership and technology to change other people's lives. Those conversations showed me that where you start doesn't decide where you end up.

Business

Client Project: CASCADE, an Operations Problem Navigator for Medline

Medline's IT teams kept fixing the same warehouse problems over and over, like the same password-access issue at the same branch, without anyone owning the pattern behind them. Our team, All Stars Associates, is building CASCADE, a single place for every warehouse disruption. Incidents arrive automatically, AI matches each one to known problems, urgent issues page the right person, and a person approves every fix, so nothing is closed silently.

As UI/UX Lead, I build the front end of the app and focus on making it clear for the real people who use it, like IT branch ops support staff, ops engineers and application owners. We built it with React, Python, SQL Server and Microsoft Fabric. See the project card.

Our Consulting Company: All Stars Associates

Our team of five (Raymond Hall, Andrew Thomas, Ben Coppage, Freeda Garcia and me) works like a real consulting firm, with defined roles: Engineering & Technical Architecture Lead, Product Business Analyst, Data & AI Lead, Project Manager, and my role, UI/UX Lead. Alongside Freeda, I also own our team's time management. Together, we set up a standing Saturday 9:00 AM team meeting to set priorities for the week, check in on completed work and work through open questions, which kept all five of us aligned on a fast-moving project.

Our vision says it best: "A world where the strongest recommendation wins because the process demanded it, not because the most confident person in the room said it first." Our motto, "Process over ego," shaped how we made decisions together.

Feedback from Mentors: Our Practice Demo

Before presenting to the client, our team ran a practice demo for our mentors, Marc and JV. They said ours was one of the most polished and professional presentations, with the strongest team introduction for setting up roles and ownership, a good balance of business and technical explanation, and the confidence of presenters who had clearly rehearsed. They also pointed out where to grow: our data model slide was too technical for a non-technical audience, a few questions got a "we'll get back to you," and we hadn't shown an actual user interface, only database wireframes.

As UI/UX Lead and the person building the front end, I took that last point to heart. We built out the real app screens and added user-flow wireframes for each persona plus a live demo, so the client could see exactly how they'd use CASCADE. I also learned to restate a question to confirm I understand it, and to give a clear timeline when I need to follow up.

Sprint 2 Showcase: Presenting to the Client

At our Sprint 2 showcase, we presented a live MVP of CASCADE: persona-based user flows from logging an incident to resolving it, a live demo, a code review and our roadmap through launch. My biggest takeaway was how much this project asked of us. My team and I worked hard and missed many life events to make it possible, and we kept the client's expectations at the top of our priorities the whole way through. That commitment is what I'm proudest of.
